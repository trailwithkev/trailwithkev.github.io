Trip at a Glance
Eight Days of Volcanoes & Lakes
Land in Guatemala City and get to Antigua — your colonial base for the first half of the trip. Hike an active lava field on Pacaya, spend a night watching Fuego erupt from 400 ft away on Acatenango, then transfer to the shores of Lake Atitlán for cliff diving in San Marcos, a sunrise hike above the lake, and last-day shopping at San Juan's artisan market.

The Guatemala Route
Fly into Guatemala City and transfer straight to Antigua — the colonial heart of the country. Use it as your base for four days of volcanoes and city life. On Day 4, transfer west to Lake Atitlán and base yourself in San Pedro La Laguna for three full days before heading back to the airport.

🚌 Get to Antigua — Bus, Uber or Shuttle
Transfer

🚌 90-Minute Bus Ride — Take Your Motion Sickness Pill
Transit
This is important: take motion sickness medication before boarding. The bus winds through mountain roads with steep curves and switchbacks for nearly 90 minutes. Don't skip this step — it can make or break the whole morning.

🌋 Hike Pacaya Volcano
Easy–Mod
The hike is not difficult — well-maintained trails, moderate elevation gain, manageable for most fitness levels. Your guide speaks decent English and sets a comfortable pace. Not an avid hiker? No problem — horses are available for anyone who'd rather skip the uphill.

Lava
When you reach the active lava fields of Pacaya, the heat radiating from the ground is intense and surreal. Most tour companies roast marshmallows over the lava vents — a genuinely unforgettable experience. Some operators cook pizza using the volcanic heat. Check what your chosen tour includes when booking.

🧙 Hobbitenango — Scenic Views & the Iconic Swing
Iconic

The iconic swing over the valley and the giant stone hobbit hand with Fuego as the backdrop are two of the most photographed spots near Antigua.

🎒 Pack Your Kit — Layers Are Non-Negotiable
Prep
Pack every layer you have: thermal base layer, fleece mid-layer, windproof outer shell, warm hat, gloves, and thick socks. Temperatures drop sharply after sunset and sleeping at basecamp elevation is nothing like sleeping in Antigua. Under-packing layers is the single most common mistake hikers make on this trek.
Pack ALL your layers — not optional Gloves + hat essential Bring snacks & 2L+ water Trekking poles: highly recommended
Late
Morn.
🚐 Tour Pickup & Drive to Trailhead
Pickup
Book via GetYourGuide, OX Expeditions, Wicho & Charlie's, or another reputable Antigua-based operator. All reputable tours include transport, guided ascent and descent, all meals (dinner, breakfast), tents, sleeping bags, and camp beds. Confirm inclusions before booking.
GetYourGuide · OX Expeditions · Wicho & Charlie's Inclusions: meals · tent · sleeping bag · bed Pickup ~10–11 AM typically
Noon
–5 PM
🌋 The Ascent — Strenuous 4–5 Hour Climb
Strenuous
The hike is strenuous — full stop. The trail gains significant elevation through dense forest before breaking out onto open volcanic scree. The altitude hits hard in the upper sections. Go at your own pace, breathe steadily, and don't race the group. By the time you reach basecamp at ~11,500 ft, you will be genuinely tired. Take it seriously: this is not Pacaya.
Strenuous — 4–5 hrs ascent Altitude affects everyone differently Go slow · breathe steady ~11,500 ft basecamp elevation
Sun-
set
🔥 Basecamp & Fuego Eruptions Begin
Eruptions
Basecamp sits at ~11,500 ft with a direct sightline to Volcán de Fuego, roughly 400 ft away horizontally. As night falls, eruptions become spectacular — massive plumes of ash and lava light up the sky every 20–30 minutes. The rumble of each eruption is felt in your chest before you hear it. Your guides will take you to ~13,000 ft for an even closer view of the active crater.
Eruptions every ~20–30 min Felt in your chest — incredible Views to 13,000 ft with guides Fuego is 400 ft away
Even-
ing
🍲 Dinner at Camp
Dinner
Your tour operator cooks a hot meal at basecamp — typically rice, beans, hot soup, and warm drinks. Simple food tastes extraordinary after 5 hours of hiking at altitude. Eat everything. Hydrate aggressively. The cold sets in fast once you stop moving, so layer up immediately.
Hot meal & warm drinks included Eat & hydrate fully Layer up immediately after arriving
9:30
PM
🛏️ Sleep at Basecamp — A Rough but Honest Night
Sleep
Sleeping at 11,000+ ft on a foldable camp bed in a shared tent is not luxury — it's adventure. The cold creeps through regardless of layers. Sleep may be light, interrupted, and strange. That's part of it. Accept the discomfort, keep your gear on inside the sleeping bag, and know that the descent and the stories make it all worth it tomorrow morning.
Camp bed in shared tent Cold, interrupted sleep — normal Keep layers on inside sleeping bag

🚐 Shuttle to Panajachel — Know Your Bus Times
Shuttle
Shuttles to Panajachel depart from fixed pickup locations in Antigua — not from your hotel door. Arrive 15–20 minutes early. Departure times: 11 AM, 2 PM, 4 PM, and 5 PM. If you're heading to any lake village other than Panajachel, take the 2 PM bus — lanchas stop running at 6:00–6:30 PM. Miss the last lancha and you're looking at a private boat costing up to 500 GTQ.
Arrive pickup point 15–20 min early Buses don't wait — no exceptions Lanchas stop 6:00–6:30 PM daily Miss lancha = private boat up to 500 GTQ All other villages → take the 2 PM bus

🤿 Cliff Diving & The 40-ft Platform — Half Day at the Lake
Cliff Dive
San Marcos is famous for its cliff jumping spots and a purpose-built 40-ft diving platform right on the lake. The platform drops you straight into the deep, clear waters of Atitlán with a volcano backdrop that makes it feel completely surreal. Spend at least half the day here — swim, jump, rest, jump again. This is the antidote to the mountain.
40-ft platform — commit or don't Spend at least half a day here Cool lake water — volcanic and clear No strong currents — safe for swimming

🍛 Lunch — Native Mayan Food in San Marcos
Lunch
San Marcos has excellent local restaurants serving traditional Mayan food — pepián (rich seed-based stew), jocon (tomatillo chicken), black bean dishes, and fresh handmade tortillas. Eat at small local spots rather than tourist-facing places. Genuinely outstanding and inexpensive.
Try pepián or jocon — traditional Mayan Eat local, not tourist menus Cash only in most places Inexpensive and outstanding

💵 Cash Reminder — Carry GTQ Before Leaving San Pedro
Tip
The further you get from Panajachel, the fewer merchants accept credit cards or USD. In San Marcos and smaller villages, Quetzales (GTQ) is the only reliable way to pay. ATMs are limited and often unreliable outside Panajachel. Withdraw enough GTQ before you leave San Pedro in the morning.
GTQ cash is king in villages Cards & USD rarely accepted off the beaten path Withdraw GTQ before leaving San Pedro ATMs unreliable outside Panajachel

Optional

The street food here is some of the best eating on the entire trip — grilled corn, chuchitos, tostadas piled high, fresh fruit with chili and lime. 🌄 Mayan Face (Indian Nose) Sunrise Hike
Sunrise
A short but genuinely steep and challenging climb above the lake. The trail rises sharply through forest before breaking out onto the rocky summit at around 7,000 ft. The views from the top are among the best in Guatemala: Lake Atitlán spread out below you, all three volcanoes visible at once, and the morning mist slowly burning off the water.

Summit

Head down to the waterfront and rent a paddle board or kayak for a few hours. The lake is at its calmest in the morning before afternoon winds pick up — the earlier the better. Paddle out into the open water with volcanoes on every horizon. Prices are very reasonable.

🛺 TukTuk to San Juan La Laguna — Local Market
TukTuk
Hop a TukTuk to San Juan La Laguna. TukTuks are the primary way to get between villages on land and cost around 10–15 GTQ. There's an important quirk: TukTuks from one town are not permitted to enter another town's territory. They will drop you at the boundary line, where a San Juan TukTuk picks you up for the rest of the way.
TukTuk: ~10–15 GTQ short distance TukTuks can't cross town boundaries Expect a handoff at the border Small GTQ coins handy for fares

🧶 San Juan Market — Textiles, Art & Local Crafts
Market
San Juan La Laguna is one of the best villages on the lake for authentic handmade goods — natural dye textiles, backstrap-loom weavings, locally grown coffee, and cacao products. Browse the cooperatives and buy directly from the artisans. Prices are fair and quality is excellent. Your last chance to pick up gifts before the trip ends.

Before You Go
Need-to-Know Tips
A few things that will make or break this trip — especially for Acatenango, the shuttle timings, and getting around Atitlán.
🛡️
Safety
Stay in well-trafficked areas, use recommended taxi services (Uber is available in Antigua), and avoid walking alone after dark.
💵
Currency
Guatemalan Quetzal (GTQ). ATMs widely available in Antigua. Cash is king at markets and all villages around Atitlán — withdraw before you leave Panajachel or San Pedro.
🌋
Volcano Tips
Acatenango requires good fitness and every layer you own — temperatures drop sharply overnight near the summit. Book a reputable guided tour only (OX Expeditions, Wicho & Charlie's, or GetYourGuide).
⛵
Atitlán Boats
Lanchas (water taxis) are the main transport between villages. Agree on price before boarding and check return schedules — lanchas stop running at 6:00–6:30 PM. Don't miss the last boat.
